## Your First-Boss Prep Checklist

Bosses in this genre rarely kill you because you lacked damage. They kill you because you arrived with the wrong loadout, no healing, and no plan for the arena. Build the following habits before you ever step through the fog.

| Checklist item | Why it matters | How to handle it early |
|---|---|---|
| Bank two or three totem sets | The first boss will test one style and punish you for having only that style | Collect relics as you explore; don't dump every totem into a single combo |
| Learn your dodge/roll timing | Rolling, sliding, and flipping are core traversal verbs — and core defensive ones | Practice the timing on low-threat creatures before a boss gate |
| Stock consumables | A first boss is a resource test as much as a skill test | Bank healing and buffs instead of using them on trash mobs |
| Scout the arena | Arena hazards and ledges decide more fights than raw DPS | Walk the perimeter before engaging |
| Test Mothbug commands | The companion is confirmed to assist in combat | Learn what it does on normal enemies so it isn't a surprise mid-fight |
| Drill plant launches and grapples | Strange plants launch you higher and further; grappling is core mobility | Practice chaining a launch into a dodge |

Treat this list as your pre-boss ritual. It takes ten minutes and saves ten wipes.

## Totems: Your Biggest Leverage in the Fight

Totems are the confirmed customization layer in Winds of Viridia: you find lost relics and treasures, then combine them to shape your playstyle. That's the single most controllable variable in the Winds of Viridia first boss encounter, because it's the part of your build you can change between attempts.

Think in roles rather than in specific items — nobody outside Shark Jump knows the totem list yet.

| Totem role | What it gives you | When to slot it |
|---|---|---|
| Sustain | Recovery or damage reduction to survive longer phases | When you keep dying in the last third of the health bar |
| Burst damage | Higher output in short windows | When you can reliably dodge but run out of time |
| Mobility | Better rolls, launches, or repositioning | When the boss's pressure keeps trapping you |
| Utility | Anything that helps you read, interrupt, or control a fight | When you don't yet understand the pattern |

A practical rule: run a *learning* loadout first (mobility + sustain) and a *clearing* loadout second (burst + sustain) once the pattern is in your head. Players who insist on a glass-cannon build from attempt one usually spend longer on the fight, not less.

## Movement Is Defense: Using Everything the Valley Gives You

Shark Jump describes combat as fast and agile, and describes traversal in the same breath — roll, slide, flip, grapple, launch. Those aren't separate systems. In a fight against a gigantic beast, your dodge *is* your traversal move.

### Read the Telegraph, Not the Health Bar

Grand bosses in this style usually broadcast with a wind-up: a shoulder shift, a raised limb, a statue's head turning. Watch the body, not your damage numbers. If you catch yourself staring at the enemy HP bar, you're already late on the dodge.

### Let the Mothbug Do Its Job

The Mothbug companion is confirmed to assist in both exploration and combat. Don't micromanage it — but do learn what it does before the boss gate so its contributions read as help rather than noise. A companion that pulls aggro for two seconds is a free punish window.

### Use the Arena as a Tool

Plants that launch you higher and further are described as exploration aids, and there's no reason to assume they stop being useful when something big is chasing you. Vertical space is usually the safest space in a boss arena.

## Reading Any Boss: A Four-Phase Framework

Since the Winds of Viridia first boss hasn't been shown, here's the framework that works on essentially every action-adventure boss — and that will make your first attempt far more productive.

| Phase | What you do | What it costs you if you skip it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Observe | Spend the first 30–45 seconds dodging and not attacking | You learn nothing and die to the same move four times |
| 2. Identify | Name the two moves that actually threaten you | You over-focus on harmless attacks and ignore the real one |
| 3. Exploit | Build your damage around the recovery windows after those two moves | Your damage is scattered and the fight runs long |
| 4. Stabilize | Fix the one mistake that keeps repeating, then push | You plateau at "so close" for an hour |

This is why the Winds of Viridia first boss is worth studying instead of brute-forcing. The framework transfers. The boss doesn't.

## Common First-Boss Mistakes

| Mistake | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Panic rolling | Chaining dodges with no read on the telegraph | Dodge once, then wait a beat and confirm |
| One-note totem builds | All offense, no recovery | Keep one sustain totem slotted until you clear |
| Ignoring the companion | Never testing Mothbug behavior | Run it against normal frenzied beasts first |
| Fighting in a corner | Getting pinned by arena geometry | Circle toward open space every phase |
| Skipping the arena walk | Getting surprised by hazards or ledges | Do one lap before you engage |
| Not changing anything between attempts | Ten identical wipes | Change exactly one variable per attempt |
