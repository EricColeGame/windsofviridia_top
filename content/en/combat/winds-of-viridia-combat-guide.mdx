## The Core Loop: Movement Is Your Real Weapon

Most action games treat dodging as a reaction. Winds of Viridia treats it as a resource you spend constantly. The game's own feature list emphasizes rolling, sliding, flipping, and grappling through a seamless world — and every one of those verbs has a combat application.

| Movement tool | Combat application | When to lean on it |
|---|---|---|
| Roll | Short invulnerability window, repositions around a beast's flank | Standard answer to a telegraphed swipe or charge |
| Slide | Low profile, covers ground fast, slips under wide horizontal swings | Crowded fights where rolling gets clipped |
| Flip | Vertical reset, breaks line of sight with ground enemies | Escaping corner traps and pack surrounds |
| Grapple | Closes distance instantly or yanks you out of danger | Re-engaging after a knockback, or escaping a wind-up |
| Plant launches | Unusual height and distance for repositioning | Reaching elevated perches mid-fight or resetting a bad angle |

The practical takeaway: treat each tool as a different answer, not as interchangeable dodges. A slide beats a roll when the attack is wide and low. A flip beats both when the danger is on the ground and you need altitude.

One more thing worth internalizing early — the environment is part of your kit. Strange plants that launch you higher or further are described as exploration tools, but they double as combat escapes and approach routes. Learning where they sit in a boss arena is often worth more than a few extra points of damage.

## Reading the Frenzy: Enemy Categories and How to Answer Them

The creatures of the valley are not random. They are affected by the frenzy, and the announcement material groups the threat landscape into a few clear buckets. Treat this table as a mental checklist the moment a new enemy appears on screen.

| Threat category | What it asks of you | Best opening to punish |
|---|---|---|
| Frenzied beasts (ground packs) | Constant lateral movement; they punish backpedaling | After a group charge whiffs — circle wide, then strike the straggler |
| Frenzied beasts (single, aggressive) | Patience; these tend to chain attacks | The recovery frame at the end of a combo string |
| Living statues | Reading slow, heavy tells | Long recovery after a slam; hit and reposition |
| Grand guardians / gigantic beasts | Endurance and phase awareness | Weak points exposed between phase transitions |

A useful habit: before you attack anything in Winds of Viridia, watch one full attack cycle without swinging. Frenzied enemies telegraph more than they first appear to, and the difference between a clean fight and a heal-heavy one is usually whether you learned the second attack in a string.

## Totems: Building a Playstyle Instead of a Stat Stick

Totems are the customization layer. Lost relics and treasures can be equipped as totems and combined to shape how you fight. Because the demo is early and the full totem pool is not public, the smart approach is to build around a role rather than chase a "best" totem.

| Build focus | What you prioritize | Plays well if you… |
|---|---|---|
| Aggressive burst | Totems that reward committing to openings | Already dodge cleanly and want shorter fights |
| Attrition | Sustain and consistency across long boss phases | Tend to take chip damage in crowded arenas |
| Mobility | Anything that extends your roll, slide, or grapple options | Prefer repositioning over perfect timing |
| Companion synergy | Effects that amplify Mothbug's contribution | Like creating windows rather than forcing them |

Two rules of thumb for experimenting:

- **Change one totem at a time.** Swapping three at once makes it impossible to tell what actually improved.
- **Test on regular frenzied beasts first.** Bosses have too many variables to isolate a totem's effect.

Community reports on optimal totem combinations are still thin, since the game is not out yet. That is actually an advantage — you get to form your own read before consensus hardens.

## Boss Fights: A Phase-Based Framework

The announcement trailer promises gigantic beasts, living statues, and great guardians of the past. Those are three very different design languages, but they share a structure you can plan around.

| Phase | What typically changes | Your job |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Boss tests you with a limited move set | Learn the tells; do not burn resources |
| Escalation | New attacks added, arena pressure increases | Identify the one attack you keep eating and build an answer |
| Desperation | Faster chains, wider hitboxes, fewer safe windows | Convert every opening; stop greed-hitting |

Three habits that separate clean boss attempts from messy ones:

1. **Fight the arena, not just the boss.** Note where plants, ledges, and grapple points sit before the fight starts.
2. **Bank one big punish per opening.** Chasing a second hit is how most deaths happen.
3. **Reset rather than recover.** If you get knocked across the arena, use the distance to breathe instead of grappling straight back into a wind-up.

If you are preparing specifically for the demo's harder encounters, run each boss twice: once to learn, once to execute. That single extra attempt is usually worth more than an hour of theory.

## Common Mistakes That Get Players Killed

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Spamming roll on reflex | Depletes your escape option when the real attack comes | Roll on the tell, not on the twitch |
| Ignoring verticality | Ground packs surround you fast | Flip or use plants to break the ring |
| Treating totems as pure damage | You lose the flexibility the system is built for | Build around a role, not a number |
| Skipping the Mothbug | You give up free distraction windows | Learn its behavior and time your commits around it |
| Over-committing after a knockdown | Recovery frames are the deadliest moment | One hit, then reposition |

The through-line in every row is the same: this game rewards players who treat stamina, spacing, and positioning as a single combined resource. This Winds of Viridia combat guide exists to shorten that learning curve, but the demo is the real teacher.
